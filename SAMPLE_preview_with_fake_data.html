Disruption Rating Rubric (Crowd Density)

Based on Professor G. Keith Still, "Crowd Risk Analysis and Crowd Safety" (Event Industry Association of Ireland). The study's key thresholds: about 2 people per m² is the planning baseline; above 3 people per m² moving crowds slow sharply (the dynamic redline); above 5 people per m² standing crowds reach the static redline, where shockwaves and crush risk begin.

LevelCrowd density (people per m²)Physical movement dynamicsRequired operational actionsTrip examples
Low Under 2.0 Individuals can walk freely. Two-way movement and self-routing are easy. Normal monitoring. Keep entrances and exits clear. Quadeca; The Jayhawks; Watsky; Indie Night; and 3 more.
Medium 2.0 to 3.0 Movement becomes restricted. Critical density is approaching, and flow capacity reaches its peak. Notify the control room. Deploy stewards to high-density zones. Prepare to redirect crowd flows. Celtics vs. Knicks; Rod Wave: Don't Look Down Tour (estimated at 2.0 to 3.0 people per m²; provisional until the venue confirms its exit plan).
High Above 3.0 to 4.5 Walking speeds drop sharply. Flow halts in stop-and-go waves, and involuntary contact begins. Upstream intervention required. Slow or pause inbound gates. Open secondary relief exits. None identified. Ratings above Medium are assigned by a person, not by the automation. Re-rate any event that draws large crowds into a single corridor.
Very High Above 4.5 The space is saturated. Shockwaves ripple through the crowd, with a high risk of progressive crowd collapse. Immediate emergency mode. Halt all inflow. Execute controlled decompression of the crowd. None identified.

Ratings use density where an estimate is available. Where it isn't, the event is rated provisionally on attendance, venue size, and closure scope. The study's static redline is above 5 people per m²; the Very High band starts at 4.5 as a more conservative trigger, following the density table this rubric was built from.
